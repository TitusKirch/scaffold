title: Workflow stubs
description: Why every workflow in the template is a thin call into kirchDev/workflows instead of a copied pipeline.

Each file in `.github/workflows/` holds a trigger and a `uses:` pointing at a
body in [`kirchDev/workflows`](https://github.com/kirchDev/workflows). The body
does the work; the stub only decides when it runs.

## Why calls, not copies

A copied pipeline is frozen at the day the repo was created: a fix made in the
template afterwards never reaches it. A call receives the fix on its next
Dependabot bump. Pasting a body back in — even one that almost fits — turns the
repo back into a copy, which is why an awkward fit is answered with an input on
the body or an own job beside the call.

## Why the pins are commit SHAs

A tag can be moved; a commit cannot. Each `uses:` is pinned to the body's
commit SHA with the version as a trailing comment, so the reference is
immutable and still readable, and Dependabot raises both together.

## Why the stubs keep some decisions locally

What stays in a stub is what the body cannot know or must not own: the
triggers, the branch filters, the permissions, and which secrets are passed.
The comments in each stub say why a trigger is shaped the way it is — for
example why `ci.yml` has no `paths-ignore` although a prose-only change cannot
break a lint.
